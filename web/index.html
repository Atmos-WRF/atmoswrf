Simulated weather

NOAA HRRR is the storm environment (Atmosphere tab, Storm environment): its run, maps, storm domain and soundings are there.

The sounding, drawn fronts, hodograph brushes and the auto outbreak are not needed: the simulated weather makes its own. Place the map centre where you want the action (Domain tab) and pick the start time; the scenario puts its cyclone so the centre is in play at the peak.

Every point of the map gets ERA5's sounding for that hour (ECMWF's reanalysis: temperature, moisture and wind from the ground up, any hour since 1940), and Atmos-WRF's storms form and grow in it with the run's physics: a past outbreak's real environment, the storms the run's own. ERA5 is a 30 km model, so it has no storms of its own: ours start where its CAPE, cap and lift let them. Needs internet, ~30 s per hour the first time, then cached. The map centre stays where you put it (Domain tab).

Quick start

How to make a storm day and watch it in GR2Analyst, step by step.

First time only

  1. Right-click the zip and pick Extract All.... Put the folder somewhere with a short path, like C:\Atmos-WRF.
  2. Double-click Atmos-WRF.cmd. The first time it installs everything by itself (10-25 minutes, a few GB). Let it finish.
  3. If Windows asks whether Python may use the network, click Allow (private networks).

NVIDIA graphics card: runs on the GPU (fast). AMD, Intel or no graphics card: runs on the processor (slower, use the 2-3 km grid).

Every time

  1. Double-click Atmos-WRF.cmd. The viewer opens in your browser.
  2. Keep the black window open while you use it: closing it stops the model.

Let the weather make the storm day (synoptic mode)

  1. Domain: put the map centre where you want the action and pick the start time (late morning local time is good: the cap breaks in the afternoon).
  2. Weather: tick Simulate the atmosphere, pick a scenario (Tornado outbreak, Squall line, Dryline day or Random weather) and when its cyclone should peak, then Preview the weather. The first preview ever builds the model's climate (~3 minutes, once); after that a new day takes ~20 seconds.
  3. Slide the Forecast hour and switch the map between fronts and isobars, temperature, dewpoint, CAPE, CIN, STP, helicity, shear, 500 hPa and Where storms can form. Sounding at a point shows the simulated sounding anywhere.
  4. Launch run: no sounding, fronts or storm starts needed. Storms form by themselves where the simulated air lets them (dryline, fronts, convergence, once the day's heating breaks the cap), each moving the way its own environment says, and the weather keeps evolving through the run: moisture return, the evening low-level jet, the trough coming out. New weather draws another day of the same kind; the run gets exactly the weather you previewed.

How it works: a two-layer quasi-geostrophic model of the jet stream makes the troughs and cyclones; a 25 km surface model over the US carries the Gulf's moisture, the hot dry air of the West and the Mexican Plateau's elevated mixed layer (the cap), with the day's heating, friction and the low-level jet. It has moist physics of its own: mid-level moisture lifted by the troughs and dried by sinking air, clouds and stratiform rain where it saturates, convection where the air is unstable and the cap gives way, rain-cooled air and cold pools, and the latent heat of it all deepening the cyclones. Where a storm patch is running, the storms' real microphysics takes over: their cold pools and rain are fed back into the weather every 5 minutes. Storms can form anywhere the weather allows (Max storms at once keeps the GPU in check). The map can show it as a surface analysis with simulated radar, a simulated infrared satellite picture or any of the fields, and Loop plays the forecast hours.

Make a storm day

  1. Atmosphere: pick a sounding from the list (or paste one, or open a file). The skew-T and numbers show what you picked. Advanced has more changes (moisture, cap, low-level jet, winds aloft); the hodograph brush paints stronger or backed winds onto parts of the map.
  2. Domain: set where on the map (or Pick center on map) and the start time.
  3. Initiation: choose how storms start:
    • tick Auto outbreak on for storms that pop up on their own, or
    • Place a cell on the map, then click where you want a storm.
    • Optional: Drag out the firing area so new storms only fire inside it (keeps the run small).
  4. Fronts (optional): draw a cold front, dryline or outflow; storms fire along it. Leave Speed empty and it moves on its own. Want a squall line? Set its Storm mode to Squall line (Auto picks a line when the shear runs along the front).
  5. Radar: pick WSR-88D, TDWR or International, click Add a radar, then click near a radar on the map. Add as many as you want.
  6. Press Launch run at the bottom.

Watch it

  • The map shows the storms: change the product (reflectivity, velocity, CC...) at the top of the map.
  • The bar at the bottom plays the run back; Live follows the newest frame. Pause / Stop are at the bottom right.
  • Tornado tracks show with their estimated EF rating; warnings (SPS, severe, tornado, PDS, emergency) are issued on their own.

See it in GR2Analyst

  1. In GR2Analyst: File → Configure Polling → Add.
  2. Paste the address shown in the Radar tab (like http://127.0.0.1:8830/poll/).
  3. Move it to the top of the list, pick one of your radars and press Start Polling.
  4. Warnings and storm tracks: Placefile Manager → + and the addresses in the Radar tab.
  5. International radars: press Add world radars to GR2Analyst once (Radar tab), then restart GR2Analyst.
  6. Friends on the same network (or Tailscale) can poll the other address shown there.

Save and share

  • Save setup... keeps everything for the run, to load again or send the file to someone.
  • Load setup... opens a saved one, or one a friend sent you.
  • Radar files (Level II) are saved in the folder output\runs.

Tips

  • Storms die fast or look smeared? Lower Scale winds (Atmosphere tab) to 0.7-0.8.
  • Not enough storms? Turn on Auto outbreak, place more cells, or draw a front.
  • Only ever get supercells? Lines need forcing: draw a cold front with Storm mode Squall line, ideally on a day whose deep shear runs along the front.
  • Something broke? Check the Console tab, or close the black window and start again.
