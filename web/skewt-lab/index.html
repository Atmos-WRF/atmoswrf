Editing

  • Drag a red dot to change temperature, a green dot to change dewpoint. Points slide along their isobar.
  • Drag a hodograph dot to change that level's wind. Hold Shift to keep the direction and change speed only.
  • Brush spreads an edit into neighbouring levels with a cosine taper over the height you pick — good for reshaping a whole layer at once.
  • Shift+click empty skew-T space inserts a level there; Alt+click a dot deletes that level.
  • Scroll over the skew-T to zoom in around the cursor (up to ×24); finer isobars, isotherms and adiabats fill in as you go. Drag empty space (or right/middle-drag anywhere) to pan, double-click to reset.
  • Ctrl+Z /Ctrl+Shift+Z undo and redo.
  • Dewpoint is clamped at or below temperature automatically.
  • Resolution (Levels tab) reshapes the sounding onto more editable points — RAOB gives the ~21 levels a radiosonde reports, BUFR gives a model-style profile every 25/10/5/2/1 hPa (up to ~950 points — zoom in to work with them). The curve is preserved; only the number of handles changes. At high resolution the dots thin out for legibility, but you can still grab any level.
  • Smooth runs one 1-2-1 pass to take the kinks out after editing. Press it repeatedly for more.

What is drawn

  • Red / green lines: environmental temperature and dewpoint. Dashed white: the selected parcel's trace.
  • Red fill is CAPE above the LFC, blue fill is CIN below it — both computed with virtual temperature.
  • The blue bracket on the left edge is the effective inflow layer (parcel CAPE ≥ 100 J/kg and CIN ≥ −250 J/kg).
  • Hodograph colours: 0–1 km red, 1–3 orange, 3–6 green, 6–9 blue, 9–12 purple. RM / LM are the Bunkers motions, MW the 0–6 km mean wind, the yellow ring is the storm motion the parameters use.

Export

  • Export SHARPpy writes the SPC plain-text format (%TITLE% / %RAW% / %END%) that SHARPpy, its sharppy.io.spc_decoder, and most SPC tools read directly.
  • Optionally the derived parameters are appended as # comment lines, which decoders ignore.

Parcel theory here follows Bolton (1980) for LCL, θe and the moist adiabat; buoyancy is integrated in log-pressure with virtual temperature. Numbers will land close to SHARPpy but not bit-identical.
